Flexible perception for ships in port

Extra eyes for ships in port

A ship in a busy port cannot always see everything around her. BeyondSea studies how sensing from outside the ship can fill in her blind spots, starting with cameras on shore.

Simulation in a virtual Port of Liverpool, twice real speed. Yellow: a craft the shore cameras have found and the ship cannot see. Green: the ship sees it herself.

The problem

Blind spots do occur

Ships moving through busy ports rely on what their crews and onboard sensors can see, and the intelligent ships of the future will rely on their sensors even more. This is usually enough. But a large vessel or a quay structure can hide a small boat, and the water close to a big ship’s own hull is hard to see from on board.

The idea

Give the ship what she cannot see for herself

Flexible perception means supplying a ship with information about the areas she cannot easily observe. Our earlier work, shown on this page, does it with cameras on shore: they detect a boat hidden from the ship and share its position with her.

Where this goes

From cameras on shore to flexible perception

The demo uses cameras at fixed places on the quay. The project asks how shore cameras, movable cameras and mobile platforms such as robot dogs or wheeled rovers can work with the ship, so that sensing can be brought to wherever a blind spot appears, much as mobile cameras already do in warehouses.

Research questions

What the project asks

  • Main question

    When a ship’s own perception of its surroundings is incomplete, how can flexible perception best supply the information it is missing?

  • Sub-question 1

    How should cameras on shore, movable cameras and mobile platforms cooperate with a ship’s own sensors, so that sensing is available wherever a blind spot appears?

  • Sub-question 2

    What kind of extra information helps a ship most, and where should movable sensors be placed, or moved, to provide it?

  • Sub-question 3

    How can methods developed in a virtual port be transferred to real hardware in a physical model port (sim-to-real), and what has to change for them to keep working?

Students will use the virtual port and a small physical model port to explore how these sources of information can cooperate, which information helps a ship most, and how methods developed in simulation transfer to real hardware.
